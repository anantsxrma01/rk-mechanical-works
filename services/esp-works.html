Air Pollution Control

How Electrostatic Precipitators protect emissions compliance.

An Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP) is an industrial gas filtration system designed to remove fine fly ash, dust, and particulate matter from boiler or kiln flue gas before release into the atmosphere. The device uses high-voltage electrostatic fields to impart an electrical charge to dust particles, which are then magnetically drawn to oppositely charged grounded collecting plates.

Mechanical rapping hammers intermittently strike the plate frames, dislodging the accumulated particulate cakes into collection hoppers below for ash disposal. Maintaining mechanical alignment, gas-tight casings, and unimpeded hopper flow is crucial for continuous environmental compliance.

Mechanical vs Electrical Scope

Focused mechanical excellence.

RK Mechanical Works specializes in the mechanical engineering aspects of ESP systems: structural steel casing fabrication, hopper manufacturing, platform and access stairways, ducting, and internal mechanical alignment during planned plant shutdowns.

Note: Electrical high-voltage commissioning and Transformer-Rectifier (TR) set electronic tuning are coordinated separately through authorized OEM/electrical specialists.

Mechanical Deliverables

Detailed ESP scope across four phases.

A. ESP Fabrication

  • Heavy structural steel fabrication for main ESP supporting columns and cross girders.
  • Fabrication of gas-tight ESP casing panels, roof panels, and penthouses.
  • Pyramidal and trough ash hopper fabrication with heating pad and vibrator pads.
  • Flue gas inlet and outlet transitional ducting fabrication with expansion joint flanges.
  • Perimeter walkways, intermediate access platforms, stair towers, and railing systems.

B. ESP Erection

  • Site foundation check, base plate grouting, and main structural bent erection.
  • Hopper hoisting, placement on support frames, and seal welding.
  • Casing panel assembly, roof erection, and continuous seal-welding to prevent cold-air ingress.
  • Erection of internal collecting electrode support beams and discharge electrode frames.
  • Installation of gas distribution screens at inlet nozzles for balanced flue gas velocity.

C. Mechanical Maintenance

  • Thorough mechanical inspection of internal collecting plates and discharge electrodes.
  • Measurement and correction of plate-to-wire electrical clearance distances.
  • Inspection and alignment of tumbling hammers, rapping shafts, anvils, and drive gearboxes.
  • Ash hopper throat inspection, knife-gate valve servicing, and anti-bridging baffle checks.
  • Detection and repair of air leaks on casing joints, inspection doors, and roof seals.

D. Shutdown Overhauls

  • Mobilizing multi-disciplinary mechanical shutdown crews during plant turnarounds.
  • Confined space cleaning and ventilation coordination prior to internal access.
  • Straightening or replacing warped collecting plates and damaged discharge wires.
  • Rebuilding worn rapping mechanisms, replacement of support insulators and shaft seals.
  • Pre-commissioning air load test readiness and mechanical sign-off.
Component Architecture

ESP assemblies and functional roles.

ESP Component Primary Role Mechanical Maintenance Focus
Collecting Electrodes Grounded steel curtains that attract and retain charged dust particles. Checking plumb, straightness, alignment, and bottom guide clearances.
Discharge Electrodes High-voltage energized wires or rigid frames creating ionization corona. Replacing snapped wires, verifying tension, and spacing centering.
Rapping Mechanisms Motor-driven hammers that strike plates to dislodge dust into hoppers. Overhauling bearings, checking hammer impact alignment, replacing shafts.
Ash Hoppers Funnel-shaped basins collecting dislodged fly ash beneath casing fields. Repairing erosion wear plates, sealing leaks, checking heaters/valves.
ESP Casing & Roof Sealed outer shell containing corrosive, hot flue gases. Welding corrosion patches, door gasket replacement, structural integrity.
Gas Distribution Screens Perforated plates at gas inlet evening out flue-gas velocity distribution. Cleaning ash buildup, repairing warped plates, clearing plugged orifices.
